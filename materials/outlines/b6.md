Part 1 covers two billing models: Athena bills bytes (per query); Redshift Serverless bills RPU-hours per second while queries run, 60 s minimum, nothing when idle, storage separate ($0.024 per GB-month managed storage); RPU = 16 GB; base default 128, range 4 to 512 (1,024 in five Regions incl us-east-1). Figure: a day timeline with query bursts: Athena charges per query blip, Redshift charges for the busy window plus 60 s minimum.
Part 2 covers the break-even from the real output (modelled): Athena Q2-style query 20 GB scanned: 10/day $34.87, 50 $152.05, 100 $298.54, 300 $884.47, 1,000 $2,935.26 per month (incl $5.57 storage); Redshift 8 RPU busy 1 h/day $95.81, 4 h/day $365.81; break-even about 31 and 123 queries a day. Busy hours are the assumption that decides it. Figure: Athena cost line vs two flat Redshift lines with crossover points.
Part 3 covers the traps: 60 s minimum (a 5 s query on an idle 8 RPU warehouse bills $0.0500 not $0.0042); open connection pools; cancelled queries billed for time run; lake still in S3 so both can coexist (decoupling). Real world: the dashboard tool keeping a connection pool open overnight (constructed).
